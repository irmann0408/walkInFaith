# CLAUDE CODE MASTER PROMPT

## Bible Adventures — Interactive Bible Game for Kids 7+

You are the lead software architect, senior Android engineer, game designer, UI/UX designer, QA engineer, and technical project manager for this project.

Your task is to design and build a polished Android game called:

# Bible Adventures: Walk in Faith

The game is designed for children ages **7 and up**.

The experience should feel like a combination of:

* An interactive Bible storybook
* A casual adventure game
* Simple educational mini-games
* A collection/reward game
* A faith-based learning experience

The game must be fun first, educational second, and always age-appropriate.

---

# 1. IMPORTANT DEVELOPMENT RULE

Do NOT attempt to build the entire game in one step.

Work incrementally.

Before writing significant amounts of code:

1. Inspect the existing repository.
2. Understand the current project structure.
3. Identify the current implementation status.
4. Create or update the project architecture.
5. Build one feature at a time.
6. Compile frequently.
7. Fix errors immediately.
8. Run tests after meaningful changes.
9. Keep the application runnable at every stage.
10. Never leave the project in a deliberately broken state.

If something is unclear, make a sensible engineering decision and document it rather than stopping unnecessarily.

---

# 2. PRODUCT VISION

The player is a young adventurer who travels through a colorful world inspired by Bible stories.

Each adventure presents a Bible story through:

**Story → Exploration → Helping → Puzzle → Choice → Lesson → Reward**

The player should feel like they are participating in the story rather than simply reading it.

There must be no combat-focused gameplay.

Instead, challenges should revolve around:

* Helping people
* Solving puzzles
* Finding objects
* Matching
* Memory
* Counting
* Navigation
* Timing
* Sharing
* Cooperation
* Kindness
* Courage
* Patience
* Faith

Primary audience:

Children ages 7+.

Secondary audience:

Parents who want their children to learn Bible stories in an interactive and positive way.

Design requirements:

* Large touch targets

* Clear visual feedback
* Friendly characters

* Positive reinforcement

# 5. ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLES

Follow these principles:

* Separation of concerns
* Single source of truth
* Unidirectional data flow
* Immutable UI state where practical
* Dependency injection where useful
* Testable business logic
* Reusable Compose components
* No business logic inside composables
* No hard-coded navigation logic scattered throughout the UI
* No unnecessary dependencies

Avoid overengineering.

The architecture should be appropriate for a small-to-medium mobile game.

---

# 6. OFFLINE-FIRST DESIGN

The game must work without an internet connection.

Core functionality must not depend on:

* Remote APIs
* Cloud services
* Authentication
* Online accounts

Store player progress locally.

Persist:

* Current chapter
* Completed chapters
* Stars
* Badges
* Scripture cards
* Character customization
* Settings
* Audio preferences

If cloud synchronization is added in the future, design the architecture so it can be added without rewriting the application.

# 9. NOAH'S ARK GAMEPLAY

Design the chapter as a sequence of short scenes.

## Scene 1 — Introduction

Show Noah and the player character.

Narrator introduces the story.

Use short dialogue rather than large paragraphs.

Example:

"Noah trusted God and followed His instructions."

The player presses:

CONTINUE

---

## Scene 2 — Find the Animals

The player explores an area and finds animals.

Use a simple tap interaction.

Animals should have friendly animations.

Examples:

* Lion
* Elephant
* Giraffe
* Sheep
* Rabbit
* Bird

Avoid frightening representations.

---

## Scene 3 — Animal Matching

Create a matching mini-game.

Goal:

Match animals into pairs.

Requirements:

* Drag-and-drop OR tap-to-match
* Visual feedback
* Positive animation
* Simple sound effect
* No punishment for mistakes

When correct:

"Great job!"

When incorrect:

"Try another one!"

Do not make children feel like they failed.

---

## Scene 4 — Gather Supplies

Create a simple collection game.

The player gathers:

* Bread
* Fruit
* Water
* Grain

Use simple tap interactions.

Avoid complex inventory management.

---

## Scene 5 — Organize the Ark

Create a simple sorting puzzle.

The player helps organize animals and supplies.

Possible areas:

* Animals
* Food
* Supplies

Use drag-and-drop.

---

## Scene 6 — Find Missing Items

Create a simple hidden-object mini-game.

The player searches the ark for several missing objects.

Objects should be clearly visible enough for children.

Avoid frustrating pixel-hunting.

---

## Scene 7 — Lesson

Show:

"What did we learn?"

Then:

"God wants us to trust Him and follow what is right."

Display a short Bible verse.

Use an appropriate Bible translation and ensure the wording/licensing is appropriate for distribution.

Do not assume that every modern Bible translation is freely licensed.

Where exact verse text licensing is uncertain, use:

* Public-domain text where appropriate
* Properly licensed text
* Or a reference-only display such as "Genesis 6:22"

* Bright

* Animal movement

Animations should support understanding rather than distract from gameplay.

# 16. CHILD SAFETY

The application is intended for children.

DO NOT implement:

* Chat
* Messaging
* Public profiles
* User-generated content
* Social media
* External advertising
* Gambling
* Loot boxes
* Cryptocurrency
* In-app purchases in the MVP
* External links accessible directly to children

Do not collect personal information.

Do not request:

* Name
* Email
* Phone number
* Address
* Location
* Contacts
* Camera
* Microphone

unless explicitly required later and properly justified.

Design with child privacy and applicable children's privacy requirements in mind.

Do not claim legal compliance automatically.
